## S1 — Profile calibration (per app)

**This section is structure and example rationale only.** Writing real bio
text per app requires the owner's own real interests/photos, which this
account does not have and should not invent. `relationship_assist.py`
already refuses (via `guard()`) anything that smells like impersonation —
writing a fake bio FOR the owner without their real material would be
exactly that, applied to myself rather than caught by the guard. What
follows is the four-app structure with a placeholder example, clearly
marked, not a real bio.

| App | What differs | Rationale (one line) |
|---|---|---|
| Hinge | Prompts-first, no swipe; 3 prompt/answer pairs | Rewards specific, story-shaped answers over a polished one-liner bio |
| Bumble | Women message first (opposite-sex matches); bio is short, scannable | Needs an opener that gives her something easy and specific to message about |
| Tinder | Swipe-first, photo-led, short bio | Bio competes with 6 photos for 2 seconds of attention — needs to be a hook, not a paragraph |
| Thursday | One-day-a-week app, higher intent, slightly longer bio tolerated | Users there have already opted into "I want this to lead somewhere this week" — bio can name that directly |

**Example structure (placeholder content — replace every bracket with the owner's real, true detail before use):**

- Hinge prompt 1: "[A real specific hobby], and the story behind it" → one real anecdote, 2–3 sentences.
- Hinge prompt 2: "[A real opinion you'll actually defend]" → something mildly contrarian and true.
- Bumble bio: one sentence naming [a real current project/hobby] + one sentence that's an easy opener (e.g. "ask me about [X]").
- Tinder bio: under 150 characters, one specific real detail + light humor.
- Thursday bio: can name "[a real thing you're looking for]" more directly than the other three.

**Photo curation** — this account does not have, and should not request, the
owner's real photos. The structural rule, not the selection: 6 photos,
app-independent, should be — 1 clear face-forward shot, 1-2 showing a real
hobby/activity, 1 social (with friends, face still clearly identifiable),
1 full-body, 0 group photos as the FIRST photo (ambiguity about which
person is the profile owner is a documented common failure mode). The
owner selects the actual files.
